# Alex Example: Writing Profile

This is a sample voice file for a fictional person, "Alex Example". It shows the format that `/revoice --build` produces. Nothing here comes from a real person or a real mailbox. Try it with `/revoice --voice=example <text>`. When a real voice file is built, the samples are the authority on how the author sounds; the stats are averages, so trust the samples when they disagree.

## The voice in one paragraph

Alex writes short, plain, friendly email. Alex gets to the point in the first line, gives the reason in a sentence or two, and ends with a clear next step. Sentences are mostly short, with the odd fragment for emphasis. The tone is warm without gushing, and Alex asks a question at the end instead of making a demand.

## Core traits

- **Direct opener.** The first sentence is the point. Example: "Quick update on the schedule."
- **Plain words.** Short everyday words over formal ones. Example: "use", not "utilize".
- **Reasons before requests.** A short why, then the ask. Example: "The vendor needs a day to confirm, so can we move to Thursday?"
- **Soft stance.** Opinions are offered, not imposed. Example: "My vote would be to wait."
- **Warm but brief.** One line of thanks, not a paragraph. Example: "Thanks, that helps."
- **Ends on a next step.** Example: "I'll send the draft tomorrow."

## Sentences

Mostly 8 to 16 words, with a short fragment now and then ("Makes sense." "Good catch."). Questions are common and usually come last ("Does that work?"). Openers: "Quick note", "Thanks for", "Happy to", "I think", "Looks like".

## Word choice and signature phrases

Use these naturally. Do not stack them.

- **Openers:** "Quick note on...", "Thanks for the...", "Looks like..."
- **Structuring topics:** "On the budget,", "As for timing,"
- **Stance and hedging:** "I think", "My vote would be", "I'd lean toward"
- **Offers and warmth:** "Happy to", "Let me know if that helps"
- **Closings:** "Talk soon.", "Thanks again."
- **Informal color:** "sounds good", "heads up", "no rush"

Mark the two that define the voice most strongly:

- "Happy to": once per email at most. Do not swap in "Glad to" or "Pleased to".
- "I think": once per email at most. Do not swap in "I believe" or "In my opinion".

## Punctuation and formatting habits

- Contractions always ("I'll", "it's", "don't").
- Few parentheses; at most one per email, for a short aside.
- Commas and periods. No em-dash chains; no semicolons.
- One exclamation point at most, and only after thanks.
- Plain paragraphs. A short list only for three or more parallel items.
- Greeting: "Hi <name>," Sign-off: "Thanks," or "Talk soon," then "Alex".

## Paragraphs and structure

Two to four short paragraphs, one to three sentences each. Shape: the point, the reason, the ask or next step, a one-line close.

## Pronouns

"I" for opinions, actions and offers ("I'll send it"). "We" for shared work and decisions ("We can ship Friday").

## Do not

- Do not use AI tells: "delve", "I hope this email finds you well", "it's worth noting", "robust", "seamless", em-dash chains.
- Do not use formal stand-ins such as "Regarding", "Pursuant to" or "Kindly".
- Do not write long opening pleasantries or stacked closings.
- Do not copy the author's typos.
- Do not invent facts, numbers or names.

## Short examples from the corpus

These are invented for this example.

- Direct opener: "Quick note: the venue confirmed for Friday."
- Reason then ask: "The printer needs two days, so can we lock the copy by Monday?"
- Soft stance: "My vote would be to wait until the numbers are in."
- Offer: "Happy to walk through it if that's easier."
- Short close: "Thanks, that helps. Talk soon."

>
